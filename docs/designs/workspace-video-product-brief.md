## Problem Statement
The founder wants a recurring-revenue product, already proven as a category, that is better than a remote workspace and a video product combined, and unique enough to monetise. Vision in the founder's words: a remote workspace with separate rooms for meetings, interviews and board meetings, opened with one click like Zoom or Teams; asynchronous video; video recording and AI summaries (Loom-style); an agentic layer; and lightweight above all: instant load, no CPU damage. Domain owned: www.workspace.video. Named competitor: cosmos.video.

## Demand Evidence
- Product level: none outside the founder. No outside person has used the product or offered money.
- Category level (other companies' evidence, not ours): Loom acquired by Atlassian for about $975M; virtual-office competitors charge roughly $6 to $20 per user per month; Cosmos claims over 1M call-hours a month (self-reported).
- Founder statement: "its already in the market ... need to be better than combined of remote workspace and a video products as well where it can be unique and monetised."

## Status Quo
The founder lived the remote-team problem first-hand. The specific tools and the specific pain were not recorded in the session (open question). What competitors claim they fix: call quality, CPU and memory use, recording length, AI summaries (Cosmos, self-reported).

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge
- First user: the founder, as a remote-team member. The person who pays (team lead, founder, ops) is not yet named.
- Wedge: not chosen. The founder chose the full platform in sequence (below) against the recommendation of a narrow first version.

## Constraints
- The founder is not a developer; the plan must be executable step by step.
- Existing asset: a hardened realtime spatial-audio engine (N=100 passes its gates; N=200 with batching and N=500 unmeasured) on LiveKit, with sign-in, workspaces, roles, invites and plan-based capacity limits.
- Absent today: video, screen share, recording, async video, AI, instant guest rooms, billing, text channels, mobile, measured browser speed.
- Speed is the stated edge, so browser CPU, memory and load time must be measured against competitors' claims (Cosmos: 10.4% CPU, 758 MB RAM, self-reported).
- Internal package and database names still say "cosmos" (the competitor's name); rename before any public work.

## Premises (all four agreed)
1. Who: the first user is the founder; the payer is not named yet.
2. Proof: the category is proven by others, the product is not; the next milestone is one real team using it daily.
3. Wedge: the full vision is a 3-to-5-year direction; the first version is one narrow thing a team pays for within weeks.
4. Speed: "lightweight and instant" is the sharpest edge, but only with measured numbers.

## Approaches Considered
- A: Fast spatial office with video and three pilot teams. Recommended; not chosen.
- B: Instant rooms plus recording and AI summaries first. Rejected: ignores the built engine and competes head-on with free incumbents.
- C: The full platform in sequence (office, rooms, video, recording, async video, AI agents). **Chosen by the founder.**

## Recommended Approach
Approach C, executed as a gated sequence: every phase ends with a real team using it before the next phase starts. This keeps the founder's full vision and removes the largest risk of C (a big build nobody has used). The concrete phase list is produced by the CEO review that follows this session.

## Open Questions
- Who pays first, and what do they pay today?
- The founder's own worst moment with current tools, in specific terms.
- Pricing unit: per concurrent user (as Cosmos states) or per seat.
- Recording: storage cost, retention, and consent rules per country.
- AI provider and cost per recorded hour.
- Guests without accounts: how they join a room by link.
- Brand: logo, company name, and replacing the "cosmos" internal names.

## Success Criteria
- One real team (5 or more people) uses it on most working days for two weeks.
- Measured browser CPU, memory and load time, published against Cosmos's claims.
- N=200 measured with batching, N=500 measured or explicitly ruled out.
- A person outside the team can join a meeting room from a link in under 10 seconds.

## Distribution Plan
Web service. The repository holds a local docker-compose (Postgres, Redis, LiveKit). No production deployment pipeline was found in the repo; establishing one is an open dependency.

## Dependencies
- LiveKit, Postgres, Redis (running locally).
- The template build plan (docs/architecture/template-driven-spaces.md, sections 13 and 14). The design review of it is paused after Pass 5; Pass 6 (phones and accessibility) and Pass 7 (open decisions) are not done.

## The Assignment
This week, talk to three people who run or manage a remote team. Ask only: what do you pay for today to talk, meet and share videos, and what annoys you most? Write down their exact words. Do not show them anything. Then write down the one person who said something that made you lean forward.

## What I noticed about how you think
- You said the product is "already in the market". You are comfortable competing where demand is proven, and that is a real strength: nobody has to be convinced the category pays.
- You chose "the full platform in sequence" over my recommendation. That is conviction, not compliance. The condition attached to it is the gate: a real team uses each phase before the next begins.
- When asked who needs it most, you answered "it is me". That is a real starting point, and it means the first job is to find the second person.
